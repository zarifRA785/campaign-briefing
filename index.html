How to read this

The words on every card

Very popular → Doing badly
How much traction a deal is getting on OzBargain now: votes, comments and clicks over the last 3 days.
Hit
At least a tenth of the comments say they bought it.
Backlash
Lots of down votes, or at least a quarter of the comments angry about the deal or the store. The product itself never counts.

Tracking 85 active and 140 archived campaigns across 5 stores, collected from OzBargain twice a day, morning and evening. In between, every 6 hours, a quick look finds new deals and checks the numbers of those in their first two days, when most deals do most of their work. Back in stock marks a deal that sold out and came back, Live again one that ended and re-opened; both are watched again.

Activity is how busy a deal is: up votes minus down votes, plus twice the comments, plus one point per 50 clicks. Votes and comments count most because they show someone cared; a click only shows they looked. In All campaigns, hover over a deal's activity or open More to see its sum; the column before it is what it gained since yesterday. On a card, each figure shows what it gained, and the line under them how much activity that adds up to. Since yesterday means since the check closest to 24 hours earlier; when that check is more than three hours off a day, the report says the hours instead.

The rating is how much traction a deal is getting now: activity a day over the last 3 days. Very popular is 50 or more a day, Popular 15 or more, Doing okay 3 or more, Not doing well half a point or more, Doing badly less. Peaked shows a deal's busiest stretch of about 3 days that we watched, when it was two or more words higher than now; where we have too little history, past its peak compares with its whole-run average instead.

What customers are saying is written by Claude from the comments; Key comments are customers' own words, except that a link shows only as the name of its site, so nothing in a quote can be clicked. A green figure beside a quote's votes, such as +5 since yesterday, is the up votes it gained: more people agreeing with it. It shows from three. The person who posted the deal is often the store or a fan, so their comments are never counted or quoted as a customer's. What the comments are about counts each topic out of every comment on the thread, so a short bar is a small share; Everything else is the rest, such as product talk, questions and chatter. A summary line with a figure or store name the code could not find in the comments it cites is marked Needs verification, with what to check. To check lists every such line with the comments it cites, to clear once a week; ticks are kept in your browser only. These lines never go in the email.

Final reviews read Finished, with how the campaign did over its whole run. One line, made by fixed rules from its curve, says how it ran: the share of its activity that came in its first 48 hours, against the days after. It ended between the last check that saw it live and the one that saw it gone, or on its planned date when we saw it live that day. The chart is its activity from posting to the close, its busiest stretch shaded and the 48-hour mark drawn in; a dashed start means we had not seen it yet.

About us gathers every comment that names Harvey Norman on a competitor's deal, and price-match talk on ours, newest first. Claude sorts each one once into a fixed kind: price match given or refused, our price compared (cheaper here, dearer, the same, or named as the alternative), stock and delivery, or other. The quotes are customers' own words.

What it means for us closes every card: one verdict and the two facts it rests on. Fixed rules decide it from the figures on the card, never Claude. On a competitor's deal: Avoid when it drew Backlash; Stock risk when a sixth or more of its comments are about stock or delivery; Worth matching when it is Very popular, or a Hit at Doing okay or better; Watch when it is busy, new or rising but not proven; No action when it is quiet. On our own deals the same rules read Fix, Stock risk, Working, Watch and Quiet. Once a campaign ends, one that was Popular or better over its run, or a Hit, reads Worth copying, or Worked when it was ours.
